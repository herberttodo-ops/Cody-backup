# Franchise Dealer Research Methodology

## Context
Research approach used for LotSignal automotive marketing software targeting franchise new car dealerships.

## Target Category Deep-Dive

### Category 1: Single-Point Franchises (PRIMARY)

**Profile:**
- Size: 1 location
- Volume: 50-150 sales/month
- New vs Used: 60-70% new, 30-40% used
- Marketing Staff: 1-2 people
- Decision Maker: Owner or General Manager

**Pain Points:**
| Pain | Why It Hurts | LotSignal Solution |
|------|--------------|-------------------|
| Compliance burden from manufacturer | Aged units get ZERO attention | Auto-surfaces aged units explicitly |
| Fear of losing allocation | Can't move new units fast = lose rights to hot models | Prioritization by carrying cost |
| Invisible aged units | Nobody has time to check which units aging | Daily feed scan + signals |
| Ads spending $ but not working | Random vehicle boosts, no aged strategy | Campaigns for aged inventory specifically |

**Red Flags to Avoid:**
- Recently losing franchise (in turmoil)
- Purely family-run with no tech adoption
- Already has manufacturer "solution"

### Category 2: 2-3 Store Groups

**Why They Work:**
- Multi-rooftop features align with product
- Higher ACV ($900/mo vs $300/mo)
- Stronger case study data (comparative)

**Challenges:**
- Longer sales cycle (multiple stakeholders)
- May need pilot at 1 store first
- Complex setup (multiple feeds)

### Category 3: Large Groups (5+ stores)

**Why Avoid for Early Access:**
- Committee decisions
- Already have processes (manual but exist)
- Too much integration complexity
- Sales cycle: 3-6 months

## Brand-Level Analysis

### Tier 1 (Prime Targets)
| Brand | Pain Level | Why Target |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| Ford | 🔴 High | Inventory mgmt notoriously difficult |
| Chevy | 🔴 High | Complex incentives, truck aging |
| Dodge/Jeep/Ram | 🔴 High | Volatile inventory, truck aging |
| Nissan | 🔴 High | Historically poor inventory mgmt |

### Tier 2 (Moderate)
| Brand | Pain Level | Why Target |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| Toyota | 🟡 Medium-High | Good mgmt BUT still have aged units |
| Honda | 🟡 Medium-High | Same - aging happens despite good turns |
| Hyundai | 🟡 Medium | Growing brand, stretched resources |
| Kia | 🟡 Medium | Same as Hyundai |

### Tier 3 (Avoid)
Luxury (BMW, Mercedes, Audi, Lexus)
- Lower volume, higher touch sales
- Different problem (aged luxury ≠ aged used)
- Higher expectations
- Smaller market

## Geographic Priorities

**Tier 1 (Competitive Heat = Urgency):**
- **Arizona** (Phoenix/Tucson): Heat aging cars quickly
- **Texas** (Dallas/Houston): Massive market, truck-heavy
- **Georgia** (Atlanta): Hyper-competitive sprawl
- **Colorado** (Denver): Mountain/seasonal patterns
- **Florida** (Tampa/Orlando): Seasonal + heat

**Tier 2 (Moderate Competition):**
- Charlotte/Raleigh, NC
- Nashville, TN
- Portland/Seattle
- Indianapolis/Columbus

**Avoid:**
- California (regulatory complexity)
- NYC metro (urban, different marketing)
- Rural markets (<50 sales/month)

## Research Step-by-Step

### Step 1: LinkedIn Search (20 min/dealer)
```
Search: "[Dealer Name]" OR "[Brand] dealer [City]"
Filters:
- Location: [State/City]
- Current company: [Dealer name or blank]
- Title: Owner, General Manager, Dealer Principal
```

### Step 2: Pain Signal Verification (10 min)
Check LinkedIn activity for:
- [ ] Posts about "aged inventory" or "aging units"
- [ ] Shares automotive marketing articles
- [ ] Comments about "slow months"
- [ ] Member of "[State] Auto Dealers" group
- [ ] Has "marketing" or "sales" in recent posts

### Step 3: Website Inspection (5 min)
Visit dealer website:
- [ ] Specials page: Promoting aged units?
- [ ] Inventory: Highest mileage/oldest listing?
- [ ] Blog: Marketing content exists?
- [ ] Offers: Generic manufacturer or specific?

### Step 4: Facebook Check (5 min)
- [ ] Active Facebook page?
- [ ] Recent posts about specific vehicles?
- [ ] Comments from customers?
- [ ] Ad transparency: Running ads?

### Step 5: Calculate Priority

**Priority Score = Pain Signals (1-5) + Accessibility (1-5) + Brand Fit (1-3)**

Pain Signals:
- 5 = Posts about aged inventory pain
- 4 = Active in dealer groups, talks marketing
- 3 = Standard dealership, no obvious pain visible
- 2 = Corporate structure, marketing overloaded
- 1 = Luxury/high-end, different model

Accessibility:
- 5 = Owner posts regularly, responds to comments
- 4 = GM active, connects with industry people
- 3 = Corporate GM, reachable through intro
- 2 = Buried in hierarchy
- 1 = Autonation/corporate (avoid)

Brand Fit:
- 3 = Ford/Chevy/Nissan/Dodge (high pain)
- 2 = Toyota/Honda/Hyundai (medium pain)
- 1 = Luxury/other (avoid)

**Outreach Priority: 9-13 first, then 6-8**
