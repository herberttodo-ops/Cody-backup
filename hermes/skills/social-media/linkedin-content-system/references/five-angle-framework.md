# 5-Angle Framework for LinkedIn Content

A structured approach to generating varied LinkedIn post angles on a single topic. Use when the user wants variety and has a specific domain (e.g., RFP writing, sales, leadership).

## The Angles

| # | Angle | Frame | Teaching Method | Example Opening |
|---|-------|-------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **The 'So What' Test** | Outcome-focused | Add "which means that..." after every claim to force value translation | "Which means that... is the most profitable phrase in proposal writing." |
| 2 | **Mirror Language** | Client-centric | Use the client's exact words from their RFP/documents | "The RFP you received contains the exact words that will win it." |
| 3 | **The Copy-Paste Trap** | Warning/stakes | Highlight costs of recycled content with specific failure modes | "80% of losing RFPs are copy-pasted from old proposals." |
| 4 | **Page One Decisions** | Urgency/timing | Emphasize early evaluator decisions with time-based hook | "Your evaluator decides in the first 90 seconds." |
| 5 | **Specificity Wins** | Concrete over vague | Contrast vague claims with specific proof points | ""We deliver exceptional results" just cost you the contract." |

## Workflow

1. **Generate all 5 drafts** — One per angle (100-150 words each)
2. **Score each 1-10** on:
   - **Hook:** Does first line earn "see more" click?
   - **Single Idea:** Is there exactly one focused concept?
   - **Value:** Would a smart reader learn something new?
3. **Iterate once** on any draft scoring below 9
4. **Select winner** based on highest average score
5. **Create 3 output versions** from winner:
   - Business Page (formal, "we" focused)
   - Personal Repost (authentic founder voice)
   - Two Comments (engagement question + soft CTA)

## Scoring Output Format

Present scores in a table for clarity:

```
| Draft | Hook | Single Idea | Value | **Total** |
|-------|------|-------------|-------|-----------|
| 1 - So What Test | 8 | 10 | 9 | **9.0** |
| 2 - Mirror Language | 7 | 9 | 8 | **8.0** |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
```

## When to Use vs. Workflow A (Iterative)

**Use 5-Angle Framework when:**
- User explicitly wants "different angles" or "variations"
- Topic is domain-specific with multiple persuasive framings
- You need to compare fundamentally different approaches

**Use Workflow A (Iterative) when:**
- User has one specific angle in mind
- Exploring freely to find the strongest single version
- Deep refinement of one concept is preferred over breadth

## Example: RFP Writing Topic

This framework was developed for OptiRFP content about RFP proposal writing. Each angle maps to a real pain point in the domain:

- So What Test → Connects features to outcomes (common failure)
- Mirror Language → Client alignment (evaluator psychology)
- Copy-Paste Trap → Efficiency vs. quality tension (workflow issue)
- Page One Decisions → Time-constrained evaluators (process reality)
- Specificity Wins → Vague claims vs. proof (writing quality)

## Quality Markers by Angle

**Strong 'So What' Test:**
- Opens with the test phrase as curiosity hook
- Shows before/after claim transformation
- Includes specific dollar/time outcome

**Strong Mirror Language:**
- Opens with client-side perspective
- Demonstrates verbatim phrase matching
- Includes evaluator quote or reaction

**Strong Copy-Paste Trap:**
- Opens with surprising stat or consequence
- Names specific tells (date errors, wrong names)
- Contrasts efficiency with lost revenue

**Strong Page One Decisions:**
- Opens with time-based urgency (seconds/minutes)
- Names the 3 signals evaluators scan for
- Shows counterintuitive structure (evidence last)

**Strong Specificity Wins:**
- Opens with vague claim as negative example
- Shows side-by-side comparison
- Includes win rate or score improvement metric
