# Evergreen Content Patterns

## Anti-Patterns Discovered

### Hardcoded Years in Examples
**Bad:** "First Time Watching [2024]"  
**Good:** "First Time Watching" (no year)  
**Why:** Years expire monthly. Andrew flagged this immediately.

### Hardcoded Movie Titles as Examples
**Bad:** "React to Dune Part Two"  
**Good:** "React to whatever is #1 at the box office this week"  
**Why:** Titles age out in weeks. Provide guidance on how to find current content instead.

## Evergreen Reference Strategy

Instead of listing specific movies, provide:

1. **Search instructions:** "Check YouTube trending page daily"
2. **Follow accounts:** List studio handles to monitor
3. **Monitor sources:** Box Office Mojo, r/movies, Netflix Top 10
4. **Seasonal angles:** "Halloween horror series (start Sept 15)"
5. **Title formulas:** "[Current #1 Movie] REACTION | First Time Watching"

## Seasonal Content Calendar (Evergreen)

| Month | Theme | Prep Window |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| September | Fall Festival (TIFF/Venice) | Monitor announcements |
| October | Halloween Horror | Start series in Sept |
| November | Holiday Blockbusters | Catch up on Oscar bait |
| December | Year-end recaps | "Best/worst of year" |
| January | Award Season | Nominations reactions |
| February | Valentine's Romance | Underrated love films |
| March-May | Spring releases | Marvel/DC slate |
| June-Aug | Summer blockbusters | Weekly reactions |

## Title Formulas That Don't Age

- "[#1 Movie] REACTION | First Time Watching"
- "[Netflix #1] REACTION | I watched this WITHOUT the trailer"
- "[Genre] Virgin REACTION | Starting [franchise]"
- "[Movie] REACTION | Was it worth the hype?"
